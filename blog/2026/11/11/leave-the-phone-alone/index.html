Leave the Phone Alone!

By Max Barrass · 11 November 2026 · 5 min read

In this essay

  • Your efficiency, my interruption
  • AI makes my time more valuable
  • Put the cost back on the caller
  • Hanging up is a complete answer

I get three to nine unwanted calls a day.

Unknown overseas number? Usually, I do not answer. If I do pick up, the caller generally has one or two seconds before I hang up.

Silence.

“This is an automa...”

“Hi...”

Click.

That is what years of this have trained me to do. Congratulations to everyone involved. You have made answering the phone feel like a mistake.

Thank you for the training

Some callers pretend to be the police, the tax office or a government fraud department. Others have a sale to make, feedback to collect or an automated message they have decided deserves my immediate attention.

I know those are different activities. A legitimate business running a survey is not the same as someone impersonating a government official. From my side of the phone, though, they compete for the same small supply of patience, and the scams have done a very good job of exhausting it.

Sometimes a human scammer gets a few words of wisdom from me before the call ends. Mostly, I just hang up.

And yes, thank you for the training. I hope I am doing well. My personal best is hanging up before “automated” is finished.

I did not set out to become good at this. I would rather be doing almost anything else.

Your efficiency, my interruption

There is something deeply disrespectful about deciding that because a call is cheap to make, somebody else should spend their time receiving it.

The caller gets scale. I get interrupted.

It does not matter that the interruption only lasts a few seconds. I still have to notice the phone, decide whether this might be important, stop what I am doing and deal with it. Then I have to find my way back into whatever I was thinking about.

Multiply that across a day. Then do it again tomorrow.

A sales target does not make my number an invitation. A customer feedback programme does not give anyone ownership of my afternoon. A pleasant synthetic voice does not turn an unwanted interruption into a service.

Calling it engagement does not mean I agreed to participate.

AI makes my time more valuable

I use AI. I build with it. I want it to take work off my hands and make room for things I otherwise would not have time to do.

That is precisely why this AI calling circus irritates me.

When machines can generate more messages, more pitches and more conversations, the scarce thing is still the human at the other end. My day has not become longer because your software can dial more numbers.

If anything, my human time has become infinitely more valuable to me. There is more I can create, investigate and finish with it. I do not want the time I save through AI handed straight back to somebody else's automated sales operation.

Before launching an AI caller, ask whether the person being called actually wants the call. If the answer is no, making the voice more convincing has not solved the problem.

Put the cost back on the caller

I want a straightforward way to say: this company has lost permission to call me. Block it. Record the complaint. Make that preference stick, including when it hires another company to make the calls.

Businesses spend plenty of money worrying about their image. Unwanted calls should be part of that conversation. If a company is genuinely responsible for a campaign, it should be willing to put its name against the way that campaign treats people.

That responsibility needs evidence. A caller claiming to represent a company does not prove that company made the call. Phone numbers can be spoofed and brands impersonated. An innocent business should not inherit a scammer's behaviour simply because the scammer borrowed its name.

So distinguish the two: verified unwanted calls from a business, and impersonation of that business. Give people a useful way to report both. Give the impersonated company a reason to help, rather than leaving the recipient to deal with every call alone.

I want accountability for the people commissioning unwanted calls, and better protection against those pretending to be somebody else. An indiscriminate public blacklist would be too easy to get wrong.

Hanging up is a complete answer

I still want the phone to work for calls that matter. An expected conversation, a call I requested or someone I actually need to hear from is welcome.

But I owe an unsolicited caller neither a conversation nor an explanation. I do not have to stay on the line long enough to discover which script, survey or synthetic personality I have been assigned today.

Hanging up is a complete answer.

If your business model depends on people being too polite to end a call they never wanted, perhaps the business model needs some work.

Use AI to save human time. Mine included.

Leave the phone alone.
