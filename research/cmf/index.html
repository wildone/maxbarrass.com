Convenience, Mystique and Unsupported Authority

A Conceptual Framework for Evidence-Sensitive Appraisal of Claims

Max Barrass, MISM · Version 4.0 · 12 November 2026

How do convenience, narrative appeal and asserted authority influence the way we check a claim? This working paper proposes the CMF framework and a programme of tests to find out whether it improves decisions beyond existing approaches.

Research status
Conceptual working paper. Not externally peer reviewed or empirically validated. No original participant study is reported.

Download PDF v4.0 (15 pages, 209 KB)

Abstract

Compelling claims can gain acceptance for reasons other than their evidential support. This paper develops the Convenience–Mystique–Fiducial (CMF) framework into a research programme for examining three potentially influential features: anticipated relief from effort or unwanted consequences, narrative appeal, and asserted authority whose relevant basis is not available for independent inspection. The central proposition is conditional: these features may encourage acceptance or transmission by reducing evidence-seeking, with scarce attention and weak accountability as candidate moderators for future testing. They are neither necessary nor sufficient indicators of falsehood. A convenient claim can be true, an engaging explanation can improve understanding, and reliance on qualified experts is often rational. The framework therefore separates persuasion cues, verification behaviour, independently assessed evidential support and downstream decisions. Existing research supplies component-level plausibility, not validation of the combined model. A bounded, estimable statistical specification is offered as a candidate rather than a universal law. The proposed programme combines construct development, controlled experiments, checklist evaluation and external validation. A practical appraisal procedure translates the theory into proportionate questions without diagnosing dishonesty or replacing substantive expertise. The intended contribution is a falsifiable synthesis and a disciplined decision aid, not a machine for detecting truth.
